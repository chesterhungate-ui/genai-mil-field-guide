Your AI toolkit.

A starting point for the AI tools on your government computer: Microsoft Copilot, Gemini for Government, Grok for Government and ChatGPT Mil. Like an OPORD shell, it gives you the structure. You fill in your own work. See what each tool does, follow a few simple rules, and try a prompt today.

My take

How to get more out of these tools than quick answers, and why it’s worth the risk of trying.7 ideas

Anything new is a risk. Changing how you already work takes effort, and you don’t know how it will turn out. There are more AI tools, courses and tips out there than anyone can use, and almost none of them are built for a specialized office like ours. This guide narrows it down to the tools we can actually open and what each one is good at.

It won’t answer every question in one swing, and nobody gets this right in a day. Treat it as a starting point. Expect to try, adjust and try again, and keep a positive outlook while you do. Most of us open these tools, type a question and stop there. Here’s what I’d do instead.

  1. The AI proposes. You approve. I believe that within 30 years, AI will handle most of our day-to-day thinking. What stays human is judgment and skill. So build the habit now: a person checks the work before anything that is expensive, public, permanent or about a person.
  2. Grok's skills and automations are the most underused feature we have. Grok can produce a finished Word document, PDF, slide deck or spreadsheet, and it can run a task every morning before you arrive. Most people never find these features, because they’re behind the + button in the chat box.
  3. Hand it the whole job, not just a question. You get a finished draft back, not just advice, and you spend your time reviewing instead of building. A chat is like calling a consultant: you get an opinion, then you still do the work. A project or agent is like an analyst at your desk: it opens your files and builds the draft for you. Sign you’re ready: you keep re-pasting the same background, or copying answers from one chat into the next.
  4. One person can build a tool the whole office uses. Do the job once by hand. When it works, save it as a custom GPT, a Gemini agent or a Grok skill so it runs the same way for everyone. Put it on a schedule only after it has run cleanly at least twice. The risk: anyone on GenAI.mil who gets the share link gets what you loaded into it, so check the files first.
  5. Give it the GIST. GIST is the four things every prompt needs. If you give the AI the gist, it can do the job:
    • Goal: what you want done, in one sentence.
    • Info: the background it needs, such as who it’s for and why.
    • Shape: what the finished product should look like, such as a one-page memo, a table or five slides.
    • Traps: what to avoid, such as jargon, guessing, or going over 200 words.
    Even a good prompt usually gets you about halfway on the first try. Read the answer and ask: is it right, is it clear, can I use it, and did it answer my question? Then fix the prompt and run it again.
    Let the AI check your GIST for you. Build a GIST Check skill in Grok once: copy the prompt and paste it into Skill creator. From then on, add the words “GIST check” to any prompt. Before Grok writes anything, it checks your prompt for all four parts, tells you what’s missing (such as who it’s for, or how long it should be) and asks you to fill the gaps. Once your prompt has everything, it does the job. You don’t have to memorize a checklist.
  6. Show it, don't describe it. Upload the actual spreadsheet, slide or memo instead of retyping it. Every time you put a file into your own words, detail gets lost.
  7. Know when it's guessing. With web search off, the AI only knows what it learned up to its training date. Turn search on for anything current, and check every citation.

My rules

Short rules for prompts that get a better answer the first time.8 rules

I adapted these from Ruben Hassid's "Claude 101" (How to AI) and rewrote them for the tools on genai.mil.

  1. One goal per prompt. Give it the goal and the context, then tell it to ask you questions before it starts.
  2. Say what "done" looks like. Length, format and audience. Otherwise it guesses.
  3. Fix the prompt, not the answer. A better prompt improves every answer after it. Arguing in the chat just piles on confusion. So if an answer misses, edit your prompt and run it again.
  4. Don't lead the witness. Ask neutral questions. If you ask "this is right, isn't it?", it will usually agree.
  5. Ask for twenty ideas, and make it rank them. The first five will be the obvious ones every AI gives. Have it put a percentage on each idea: how likely a typical AI would be to suggest it. Then look hard at the low-percentage ones. That’s where the new thinking is. Use this prompt.
  6. Ask for the real file. If you need a deck or a spreadsheet, have Grok build it, not describe it.
  7. One project per line of effort. Keep memory short and accurate, and never let it store PII.
  8. Start fresh when it drifts. When a long chat gets worse, ask for a summary and paste it into a new chat.

Grok

Grok: build a GIST Check skillTurn on Skill creator and paste this. After that, end any prompt with “GIST check.” In ChatGPT Mil, you can paste it into Custom instructions instead.

Then ask me up to three questions to fill the gaps, and show a better version of my prompt with the gaps marked [like this]. Don't start the task until I answer or say "go." If all four parts are clear, say "GIST complete" and do the task. Ask me any questions you need before you build it.

Where to find them

genai.mil for Gemini, Grok and ChatGPT Mil. Teams and Outlook for Copilot.2 places

genai.mil

Open genai.mil on your government computer and sign in with your CAC. This one page is where you pick Gemini, ChatGPT Mil or Grok.

The menu across the top has About, Training, Events and Resources. Start with Training.

You'll also see an API key setting. That is for approved developers only, and you don't need it to use the tools.

Copilot

Copilot is not on genai.mil. In Teams, click the Copilot tab on the far left. It is also in Outlook.

It opens as a simple chat. You can start new chats, go back to recent ones, and change settings.

Pick a tool in 10 seconds

What each tool is best at, on one card each.4 cards

Copilot

Best for: quick edits, and summarizing your own meetings, chats and email.

  • Summarizes your Teams meetings, chats, email and documents
  • Finds files and people you already have access to
  • Pulls out action items and key points
  • No internet and no images in the government version

Where: the Copilot tab on the left side of Teams, or in Outlook. Not on GenAI.mil.

ChatGPT Mil

Best for: writing documents and building your own helper.

  • Custom GPTs: your own assistant, loaded with your files
  • Explore GPTs to use what others built
  • Projects keep files, chats and memory together
  • Helper GPTs coach your prompts

Where: GenAI.mil, since 31 Aug 2026

Gemini

Best for: long research, big stacks of documents, and building agents.

  • Reads documents, images, audio, video and handwritten notes
  • Takes a huge pile of files at once
  • Deep Research writes long reports with citations
  • Agent Designer, with manager agents and schedules

Where: GenAI.mil, since 9 Dec 2025

Grok

Best for: making real files and running tasks on a schedule.

  • Skills make Word, PDF, PowerPoint and Excel files
  • Automations run tasks daily or weekly
  • Team spaces share projects and skills
  • Expert mode runs code, but expect waits

Where: GenAI.mil, since 31 Aug 2026

Free training on genai.mil

Where to find the free courses, help pages and live events.5 steps

There's free training for all three genai.mil tools: Gemini, ChatGPT Mil and Grok. It takes a few clicks to find.

  1. Open genai.mil and sign in with your CAC.
  2. Click Training in the top menu. Pick a tool to see its self-paced courses, videos and quick references.
  3. Use the links there to each tool's own help pages, such as help.openai.mil for ChatGPT Mil and docs.grok.genai.mil for Grok. They only open on the government network.
  4. Click Events to register for a live workshop or office hours for more hands-on practice.
  5. Spend ten minutes clicking around. Most of what's on this page, you'll find there too.

Words you'll hear, in plain English

AI terms you’ll hear, one line each.26 terms
AI modelThe engine behind the chat box. Gemini, GPT and Grok are models from three different companies.
PromptWhat you type to the AI. Say who it's for, what you want and the format.
GPTShort for Generative Pre-trained Transformer, OpenAI's name for the model behind ChatGPT.
Custom GPTYour own version of ChatGPT, set up once with instructions and files for one job. Only ChatGPT Mil has these.
Everyone can chatA ChatGPT Mil setting that makes a share link anyone on GenAI.mil can use. Remove sensitive files first.
Reasoning effortHow hard the AI thinks before it answers. Higher is slower but more careful.
AgentAn assistant that carries out several steps on its own, such as reading notes and drafting a summary.
Manager agentA Gemini agent that splits a job and hands the pieces to other agents, like a project manager.
Knowledge fileA file you attach so the AI answers from your material, such as an SOP or a spreadsheet.
RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)The AI looks up the right passages in your files before it answers, like an open-book test.
Deep researchThe AI spends several minutes searching and reading, then writes a long report with citations.
MultimodalThe AI can take in pictures, scans, audio and video, not just text.
Project or workspaceA folder that keeps related chats, files and instructions together.
Team spaceA shared area in Grok where colleagues use the same projects and skills.
AutomationA task the AI runs by itself on a schedule, such as every weekday at 0700.
Custom instructionsStanding rules the AI follows in every chat, such as “lead with the bottom line.”
MemoryThe AI remembers facts about you between chats. Check what it saved, and never let it store PII.
ConnectorA plug that lets the AI read from a system you already use, such as email or a shared drive.
SkillA saved recipe for one kind of job, such as “make a PowerPoint,” that the AI follows the same way every time.
Token and context windowA token is a small piece of a word. The context window is how much the AI can read at once.
SandboxA walled-off computer the AI uses to run code and build files without touching anything else.
Training cutoffThe date the AI stopped learning. Turn on web search for anything newer.
Web search indexThe copy of the internet a tool searches. It may be a few hours behind the live web.
CitationA link to where the answer came from. Click it and check it.
HallucinationWhen the AI states something false with confidence. That's why a person checks every output.
CUI and IL5CUI is sensitive but unclassified information. IL5 is the security level genai.mil is approved for: it covers CUI, not classified.
