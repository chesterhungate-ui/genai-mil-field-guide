Four AI tools on your government computer. Here's what each one is best at.

A one-page field guide to Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT Mil, Gemini for Government and Grok for Government. Open the tool, copy a seed prompt, and try it yourself.

The one rule: unclassified only. CUI is allowed on GenAI.mil. No PII, no health information, nothing classified. A person checks every output before it leaves the office.

Pick a tool in 10 seconds

Copilot

Best for: your own email, documents and Teams.

  • Lives inside Microsoft 365 on your DoD account
  • Summarize threads, draft replies, catch up on meetings

Where: Microsoft 365 (DoD), not GenAI.mil

ChatGPT Mil

Best for: writing and rewriting documents.

  • Projects keep files and chats together
  • Custom GPTs for tasks you repeat

Where: GenAI.mil, since 31 Aug 2026

Gemini

Best for: research and building agents.

  • Answers grounded in Google Search
  • Agent Designer: build an assistant in plain English

Where: GenAI.mil, since 9 Dec 2025

Grok

Best for: thinking a problem through.

  • Auto, Fast and Expert reasoning modes
  • Workspaces, projects and reusable playbooks

Where: GenAI.mil, since 31 Aug 2026

Seed prompts: try each tool on its strength

Every example is notional. Swap in your own unclassified details, never names or PII.

Copilot: catch up on an email threadOpen the thread in Outlook, then ask Copilot.
Summarize this thread for a busy leader. Give me a two-sentence bottom line up front, then a list of every tasker with the owner's role and the due date. Flag anything that is still undecided. Keep it under 150 words.
ChatGPT Mil: turn rough notes into a memoStart a Project, paste your notes, run this.
You are a staff writer. Turn the notes below into a one-page information memo. Use a bottom line up front, then three to five numbered points, then a recommendation. Plain English, active voice, sentences under 25 words. If something is missing, list the questions I need to answer instead of guessing.

Notes: Range day moved from Tuesday to Thursday because of weather. S4 to confirm bus support by Monday. Company A needs 40 more sets of eye protection. Decision needed: land navigation refresher half day or full day.
Gemini: research with sourcesUse a public topic so the search can help.
Find the most recent public guidance on running effective after action reviews in a small unit. Summarize it in five bullets a new team leader can use tomorrow. Give the source and date for each bullet, and tell me which points you could not confirm.
Gemini: build your first agentPaste into Agent Designer's description box.
Create an agent called "Meeting Notes to Action Items." It turns rough, unclassified meeting notes into a one-page summary: the meeting purpose in one sentence, decisions made, a table of tasks (task, owner by role, due date, status) and open questions. It uses only the notes provided, marks missing details as TBD, and never includes names or personal information.
Grok: think a decision throughSwitch to Expert mode first.
Help me compare two options for a notional office move. Option A: move all at once over one weekend. Option B: move one section per week for four weeks. Build a short decision matrix on mission impact, cost, risk and workload. Then argue against your own recommendation in three sentences and tell me what information would change your answer.
All four: same prompt, side by sideRun this in each tool and compare the answers.
In 120 words or less, explain to a new staff officer what CUI is, what it is not, and one common mistake people make when handling it. End with one question I should ask my security manager.

Try it in 3 steps

  1. On your government computer, open the tool: genai.mil with your CAC, or Copilot in Microsoft 365.
  2. Copy one seed prompt above and paste it in.
  3. Check the answer. Fix one thing in the prompt and run it again.
