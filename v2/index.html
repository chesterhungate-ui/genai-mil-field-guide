Start small, then build one tool your office uses

4 steps · 5 habits
  1. Step 1Run one promptA seed prompt on a real, unclassified task.
  2. Step 2Hand it the whole jobUpload the real files. Get a draft back.
  3. Step 3Save it as a toolA Grok skill, Gemini agent or custom GPT.
  4. Step 4Put it on a scheduleOnly after it runs clean twice.

New is a risk, and nobody gets this right in a day. Expect to try, adjust and try again.

Five habits that keep the work right

The AI proposes. You approve.A person checks anything expensive, public, permanent or about a person.
Show it, don’t describe it.Upload the real file instead of retyping it.
Fix the prompt, not the answer.Edit and rerun. Arguing in the chat adds confusion.
Don’t lead the witness.Ask neutral questions, or it will just agree.
Know when it’s guessing.Turn on web search for anything current. Check citations.

Give every prompt the GIST, and let Grok check it

4 parts
  1. GGoalWhat you want done, in one sentence.
  2. IInfoWho it’s for, and why.
  3. SShapeThe finished product: a memo, a table, 5 slides.
  4. TTrapsWhat to avoid: jargon, guessing, over 200 words.
  5. ✓Add “GIST check”Grok lists what’s missing before it writes. Get the skill

Then work in a loop

Prompt→Read the answer→Fix the prompt→Run it again↻

18 prompts · 5 tabs

Every example is notional. Use unclassified details, never names or PII.

Tighten a Teams message or emailPaste your draft into the Copilot tab in Teams.
Rewrite my draft below so it is shorter and clearer for a senior leader. Put the ask in the first sentence. Keep it professional and under 80 words. Give me two versions: one formal, one casual for a Teams chat.

Draft: Hey sir, wanted to touch base on the training schedule thing from last week, we still need a decision on whether land nav is a half day or a full day because S4 needs to lock in buses by Monday and I think a half day is probably fine but wanted your thoughts.
Pull taskers from a file in your cloudAdd the file as a source first: a cloud file from Teams or OneDrive.
From the attached file, list every tasker with the owner's role and the due date, in a table. Then give me a two-sentence bottom line for my boss. Flag anything with no owner or no date.
Research with sourcesUse a public topic so the search can help.
Find the most recent public guidance on running effective after action reviews in a small unit. Summarize it in five bullets a new team leader can use tomorrow. Give the source and date for each bullet, and tell me which points you could not confirm.
Build your first agentPaste into Agent Designer's description box.
Create an agent called "Meeting Notes to Action Items." It turns rough, unclassified meeting notes into a one-page summary: the meeting purpose in one sentence, decisions made, a table of tasks (task, owner by role, due date, status) and open questions. It uses only the notes provided, marks missing details as TBD, and never includes names or personal information.
Turn messy notes into a trip reportUpload your notes. A photo of handwritten notes works too.
Turn the attached notes into a trip report I can download as a Word document. Use these headings: purpose, key engagements, observations, decisions, action items (a table with task, owner by role and due date) and follow-ups. Use only what is in my notes, mark anything missing as TBD, and leave out names and personal information.
Deep research on your own filesChoose Deep Research, attach several unclassified documents, then run this.
Read all the attached documents and write a report on what they agree on, where they conflict, and what is missing. Cite the document and section for every point, so I can check it. End with three questions I should ask before making a decision.
A manager agent with a small teamAgent Designer. Then use the Preview tab to test it before you share it.
Create a manager agent called "Staff Product Builder." When I give it a topic and unclassified source files, it assigns the work to three sub-agents: a researcher that pulls the key facts from the files, an analyst that lists options with pros, cons and risks, and a writer that turns the result into a one-page information paper with a bottom line up front. The manager checks that every fact traces back to a source file and flags anything it can't confirm.
Build a GIST Check skillTurn on Skill creator and paste this. After that, end any prompt with “GIST check.” In ChatGPT Mil, you can paste it into Custom instructions instead.
Help me build a skill called "GIST Check." Use it whenever my prompt includes the words "GIST check." Before doing any work, check my prompt for four parts: Goal (what exactly I want done), Info (the background, including who it's for), Shape (the format and length, such as one page, a table or five slides) and Traps (what to avoid). Reply first with a short table that lists each part as Clear or Missing, with one line on what's missing, such as "no audience" or "no length." Then ask me up to three questions to fill the gaps, and show a better version of my prompt with the gaps marked [like this]. Don't start the task until I answer or say "go." If all four parts are clear, say "GIST complete" and do the task. Ask me any questions you need before you build it.
Make a real slide deckClick + in the chat box, add the PowerPoint skill, then run this.
Make a five-slide PowerPoint for a 10-minute leader brief on a notional office move. Slide 1: title and bottom line. Slide 2: why we're moving. Slide 3: the timeline as a simple table. Slide 4: risks and how we reduce them. Slide 5: decisions needed from the commander. Plain style, large text, no more than four bullets a slide.
Set up a daily automationOpen a project, choose Automations, and use this as the task.
Every weekday at 0700, search the web for public news from the last 24 hours about [a public topic your section tracks]. Write a half-page roundup: a two-sentence bottom line, then up to five bullets, each with its source and date. End with one line on anything that may need a leader's attention. Use public sources only.
Teach it your format onceSkill creator. Attach one good, unclassified example of the product first.
Help me build a skill called "Our Memo Format." Use the attached example as the template: copy its headings, order, length and tone exactly. When I give it rough notes, it fills in that format, keeps every fact from my notes, marks anything missing as TBD, and adds nothing I didn't provide. Ask me any questions you need before you build it.
Analyze a spreadsheetSwitch to Expert mode and upload an unclassified Excel or CSV file. It may take a few minutes.
Analyze the attached spreadsheet. Tell me in three sentences what it shows, then list the three biggest trends or outliers with the numbers behind them. Build one chart that shows the most important trend, and give me a cleaned-up Excel file I can download. Tell me any data problems you found, such as blanks or duplicates.
Turn rough notes into a memoStart a Project, paste your notes, run this.
You are a staff writer. Turn the notes below into a one-page information memo. Use a bottom line up front, then three to five numbered points, then a recommendation. Plain English, active voice, sentences under 25 words. If something is missing, list the questions I need to answer instead of guessing.

Notes: Range day moved from Tuesday to Thursday because of weather. S4 to confirm bus support by Monday. Company A needs 40 more sets of eye protection. Decision needed: land navigation refresher half day or full day.
Build a custom GPT for your work streamPaste into the custom GPT's instructions box, then add your unclassified reference files.
You are the go-to helper for a notional battalion S3 training section. Answer questions using the attached reference files first, and say which file each answer came from. If the files don't cover the question, say so plainly and don't guess. Keep answers short: a bottom line up front, then up to five bullets. When someone asks for a product, such as a training schedule or a memo, ask up to three questions before you draft it. Never store or repeat names, phone numbers or other personal information.
Set your custom instructions onceSettings, then Personalization, then Custom instructions.
I am a staff officer. Write in plain English for busy leaders. Always start with a one or two sentence bottom line up front. Use short numbered points instead of long paragraphs. If you are not sure about something, tell me instead of guessing, and list what I should check.
Shape an idea into a one-page conceptSet reasoning to High first. Good for early concepts and requirements, not code.
Help me turn a rough idea into a one-page concept for my boss. The idea: a shared, unclassified tracker so every section can see upcoming training, room bookings and vehicle requests in one place. Write it with these headings: Problem, Proposed solution, Who uses it, What success looks like in 90 days, Risks, and Decisions needed. Ask me up to three questions first if something important is missing.
Twenty ideas, ranked by how obvious they areWorks in any tool. Replace the bracket with your problem.
Give me 20 ideas for [your problem, for example: getting more junior leaders to use our office's AI tools]. For each idea, estimate the percent chance that a typical AI assistant would suggest it, from 0 to 100 percent. Sort the list from most likely to least likely. At least eight ideas should be under 10 percent. Then pick the two low-percentage ideas you think are most worth trying, and tell me in one sentence each why.
Same prompt, side by sideRun this in each tool and compare the answers.
In 120 words or less, explain to a new staff officer what CUI is, what it is not, and one common mistake people make when handling it. End with one question I should ask my security manager.

26 AI terms in plain English
AI modelThe engine behind the chat box. Gemini, GPT and Grok are models from three different companies.
PromptWhat you type to the AI. Say who it's for, what you want and the format.
GPTShort for Generative Pre-trained Transformer, OpenAI's name for the model behind ChatGPT.
Custom GPTYour own version of ChatGPT, set up once with instructions and files for one job. Only ChatGPT Mil has these.
Everyone can chatA ChatGPT Mil setting that makes a share link anyone on GenAI.mil can use. Remove sensitive files first.
Reasoning effortHow hard the AI thinks before it answers. Higher is slower but more careful.
AgentAn assistant that carries out several steps on its own, such as reading notes and drafting a summary.
Manager agentA Gemini agent that splits a job and hands the pieces to other agents, like a project manager.
Knowledge fileA file you attach so the AI answers from your material, such as an SOP or a spreadsheet.
RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)The AI looks up the right passages in your files before it answers, like an open-book test.
Deep researchThe AI spends several minutes searching and reading, then writes a long report with citations.
MultimodalThe AI can take in pictures, scans, audio and video, not just text.
Project or workspaceA folder that keeps related chats, files and instructions together.
Team spaceA shared area in Grok where colleagues use the same projects and skills.
AutomationA task the AI runs by itself on a schedule, such as every weekday at 0700.
Custom instructionsStanding rules the AI follows in every chat, such as “lead with the bottom line.”
MemoryThe AI remembers facts about you between chats. Check what it saved, and never let it store PII.
ConnectorA plug that lets the AI read from a system you already use, such as email or a shared drive.
SkillA saved recipe for one kind of job, such as “make a PowerPoint,” that the AI follows the same way every time.
Token and context windowA token is a small piece of a word. The context window is how much the AI can read at once.
SandboxA walled-off computer the AI uses to run code and build files without touching anything else.
Training cutoffThe date the AI stopped learning. Turn on web search for anything newer.
Web search indexThe copy of the internet a tool searches. It may be a few hours behind the live web.
CitationA link to where the answer came from. Click it and check it.
HallucinationWhen the AI states something false with confidence. That's why a person checks every output.
CUI and IL5CUI is sensitive but unclassified information. IL5 is the security level genai.mil is approved for: it covers CUI, not classified.
